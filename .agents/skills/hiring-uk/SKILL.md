# Hiring (UK)

The first hire is a Phase 4 decision. Before then, the founder does everything — that is not
a failing, it is how the unit economics get genuinely understood. Hire to remove a proven
bottleneck, not to feel like a "real" business.

## Before hiring anyone
- Is the task repeatable and documented? If not, write the SOP first (see `ops-sops`) — you
  cannot delegate what you cannot describe.
- Can it be automated instead? Automation has no payroll, no holiday, and no NI.
- Does the revenue support it? A hire is a large fixed cost — model it with
  `financial-modeling-uk`.

## Contractor vs employee
- **Contractor / freelancer** — flexible, no employer NI or pension, good for specialist or
  variable work. Mind IR35 and employment-status rules — a contractor treated like an
  employee is an employee in HMRC's eyes.
- **Employee** — for core, ongoing work. Brings employer obligations: PAYE, employer
  National Insurance, pension auto-enrolment, holiday, statutory rights, and employers'
  liability insurance.
- Most first "hires" should be contractors engaged for specific deliverables.

## If hiring an employee
1. Register as an employer with HMRC; set up PAYE payroll.
2. A written contract and a clear role. Right-to-work checks are mandatory.
3. Auto-enrolment pension; employers' liability insurance.
4. Budget the true cost: salary + roughly 15% employer NI (rate from 6 April 2025 — verify on gov.uk) + pension + tools + management time.

## The honest framing
A hire not backed by revenue shortens the runway and raises the stakes. Verify the maths,
and confirm employment-law specifics with a UK accountant or HR adviser — this skill is
information, not advice.
