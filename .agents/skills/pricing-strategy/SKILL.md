# Pricing Strategy

Pricing is the fastest lever on profit and the most under-tested. Most founders price too
low out of fear. This skill makes pricing an evidence-based decision.

## The three reference points
1. **Cost-plus** — the floor. The price must clear COGS, card fees, carrier, returns, and a
   margin. Cost-plus tells you the minimum, never the answer.
2. **Competitor-anchored** — what the market has trained customers to expect. Document 3+
   comparables with exact prices and the date checked.
3. **Value-based** — what the outcome is worth to the customer. This is the ceiling and the
   real target. Anchor to the cost of the problem, not the cost of the product.

## Testing willingness to pay
- The only real WTP test is a price tag and a card field. In interviews ask: "at what price
  would this be so expensive you would not buy it? So cheap you would doubt it?"
  (Van Westendorp).
- The strongest evidence: ≥ 3 customers said yes to the exact price (see `evidence-bar`).
- A smoke test with a real "buy" button beats any survey.

## UK specifics
- Decide VAT-inclusive vs exclusive display. Consumer prices are shown VAT-inclusive; model
  the margin hit of crossing the £90k threshold (see `financial-modeling-uk`).
- Charm pricing (£29 vs £30) is real but small — do not let it distract from the price band.

## Method
1. Establish the floor (cost-plus) and the comparables band.
2. Propose a value-based price above the band, with the reasoning.
3. State the WTP evidence and tag gaps `[ASSUMPTION — H/M/L]`.
4. Feed the chosen price into `financial-modeling-uk` and confirm LTV/CAC still clears 3.

## Anti-pattern
Pricing low to "reduce risk" raises risk — it destroys margin, attracts the worst customers,
and signals low quality. When unsure, test the higher price first.
