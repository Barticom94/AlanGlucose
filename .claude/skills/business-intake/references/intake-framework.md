# Business Idea Intake Framework

> Fill this in as fully as you can. It transfers your whole picture of the idea to the brain,
> which uses it to seed every state file and start Phase 0.
>
> **Rules of the road:**
> - Honesty over polish. This is a diagnosis, not a pitch. A weak answer you know is weak is
>   far more useful than a confident answer that is guessed.
> - "I don't know" is a valid answer — write it. Gaps get probed next, not judged.
> - Everything here is treated as your *belief*, not fact. The brain tags it all as an
>   assumption, and Phase 1 tests it. You are seeding hypotheses, not stating truths.
> - Fill it at your own pace, even offline. Paste it back when done; the brain then probes
>   the gaps and the vague answers before anything is locked in.

---

## 1. The idea & the insight

**1.1 The one-paragraph pitch.** What is the idea? Who is it for, what do you sell them, why
do they pay? One paragraph — if you cannot, it is not clear enough yet, and that is a finding.
> {{ }}

**1.2 What you'll actually sell.** A physical product, a digital product, a service, a
marketplace? Describe the actual thing a customer receives.
> {{ }}

**1.3 What stage are you at?** Just an idea / sketched it out / talked to people / built
something / already selling. Be specific about what exists today.
> {{ }}

**1.4 The unique insight.** What do you understand about this — the customer, the market, the
problem — that most people don't? What did you see or experience that made this idea obvious
to you when it is not obvious to others? *(This is the most important question here.)*
> {{ }}

## 2. Problem & customer

**2.1 The problem, in the customer's words.** Not "people need X" — the actual pain, the way
a customer would describe it. How often does it bite? How much does it sting?
> {{ }}

**2.2 The job they're hiring you to do.** Beyond the functional task — what is the customer
really trying to achieve? The functional job, the social job (how it makes them look), and
the emotional job (how it makes them feel).
> {{ }}

**2.3 The beachhead customer.** The ONE narrow group to win first. Who exactly are they —
their situation, where they spend time, how you could reach them? "UK consumers" is not a
beachhead.
> {{ }}

**2.4 How they cope today.** The current workaround or competitor. What does it cost them in
money, time, and frustration? If there is no current solution, ask whether the problem is real.
> {{ }}

**2.5 Why now.** What changed — technology, regulation, behaviour, cost — that makes this
possible or urgent now, and not three years ago?
> {{ }}

**2.6 Rough size.** Napkin maths: roughly how many people are in your beachhead? At a rough
price, what would that beachhead be worth if you won a sensible share of it? It only needs to
be big enough to be worth your time — not a billion-pound market.
> {{ }}

## 3. The model

**3.1 How it makes money.** What does the customer pay for, and how — one-off, subscription,
per-use? Any secondary revenue?
> {{ }}

**3.2 Pricing.** What would you charge, and why that number? What is it anchored to — your
cost, competitors, or the value to the customer?
> {{ }}

**3.3 The buying decision.** Who decides to buy? Who pays? Who else influences or could block
the decision? For a gift, a household purchase, or a business sale, these are different people.
> {{ }}

**3.4 Acquisition gut-check.** Roughly, how would a customer first hear about you, and what
might it cost to get one? Is that plausibly less than what a customer is worth to you over
time? A rough instinct is fine — this is the maths that quietly kills most ecommerce ideas.
> {{ }}

**3.5 Channels.** How will customers find you? List every channel — and mark any you already
have access to (an audience, a list, a network, a platform, a relationship).
> {{ }}

**3.6 Sourcing & fulfilment (rough).** High level only: where would the product come from, and
how would it reach the customer? Detail comes much later — a sketch is enough now.
> {{ }}

**3.7 Numbers you already believe.** Any figures already in your head — price, costs, margin,
market size, conversion rate. List them. They will be tagged as assumptions and tested.
> {{ }}

## 4. Competition & edge

**4.1 Who else does this.** Direct competitors, indirect ones, and the "do nothing / DIY"
option — often the real competitor. Name them. "No competitors" usually means "I haven't looked".
> {{ }}

**4.2 Why you.** Why are you the right person to build this? What do you bring that a random
founder would not?
> {{ }}

**4.3 Why it would be hard to copy.** *Optional at this stage.* If a competitor with money and
12 months tried to beat you, what would protect you? "Nothing yet" is a perfectly honest answer
for an early idea — leave it blank if so.
> {{ }}

## 5. You, the founder

**5.1 Time.** Realistically, how many hours a week can you give this, and when?
> {{ }}

**5.2 Money.** How much can you afford to invest, and how much can you afford to *lose*
without it hurting? What is covering your living costs meanwhile?
> {{ }}

**5.3 Skills & experience.** What relevant skills and experience do you bring? What are the
obvious gaps?
> {{ }}

**5.4 Assets you already hold.** Anything that gives you a head start — an audience, an email
list, supplier contacts, tools or accounts already set up, capital, domain expertise, a
relevant network.
> {{ }}

**5.5 Risk appetite.** How much are you genuinely willing to risk, and how would you feel if
this failed? Is the day job staying?
> {{ }}

**5.6 What worries you most.** Honestly — what is the part of this idea that keeps nagging at
you? (Gut feeling here; the analytical version is the capstone question at the end.)
> {{ }}

## 6. The goal

**6.1 Ambition.** Which is this — a lifestyle business (steady income, stays small), a scale
business (grow it large), or a raise-and-exit? No wrong answer, but it changes every decision
downstream.
> {{ }}

**6.2 What success looks like in 12 months.** A sketch, not a forecast — the shape of success,
not precise numbers. What would make you say "this is working"?
> {{ }}

**6.3 Kill criteria.** What would make you stop? "If X is not true by date Y, I walk away."
Decide this now, while you are clear-headed.
> {{ }}

## 7. Working with the brain

This section is not about the business — it is about how you want the brain to work with you.

**7.1 Your biggest knowledge gaps.** Which parts of building a business do you feel least
confident about (finance, marketing, legal, operations, etc.)? The brain will scaffold these
more heavily.
> {{ }}

**7.2 Challenge vs encouragement.** How hard do you want to be pushed? The brain defaults to
blunt and critical — say if you want it harder still, or slightly softened.
> {{ }}

**7.3 Decision style.** Do you prefer one clear recommendation, or the options laid out? Fast
and decisive, or slow and thorough?
> {{ }}

**7.4 Hours and rhythm.** How many hours a week (from 5.1), and on what rhythm — daily small
sessions, or one big weekly block? When would your weekly review sit?
> {{ }}

**7.5 What you most want help with.** If the brain could take three things off your plate,
what would they be?
> {{ }}

---

## Capstone — your riskiest assumption

You have just laid the whole idea out. Now the most important question.

**Of everything above, what is the single assumption that — if it turned out to be wrong —
would kill this idea?**

> {{ }}

That assumption is what Phase 0 and Phase 1 test first. Everything else waits behind it.
