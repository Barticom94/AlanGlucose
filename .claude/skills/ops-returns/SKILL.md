# Operations — Returns & Refunds

In UK ecommerce, returns are a legal obligation with hard deadlines — not a goodwill
gesture. Get the policy right before launch.

## UK consumer law — the non-negotiables
- **Consumer Contracts Regulations 2013** — for distance (online) sales, the customer has a
  14-day cooling-off period to change their mind, starting when they receive the goods. If
  you fail to inform them of cancellation rights, that period extends up to 12 months.
- Refunds must be issued within 14 days of getting the goods back (or proof of return). You
  must refund the basic delivery cost too.
- **Consumer Rights Act 2015** — goods must be of satisfactory quality, fit for purpose, and
  as described. A faulty item carries a 30-day short-term right to reject for a full refund.
See `@docs/UK-LEGAL-TAX.md` and the `legal/` folder. This is information, not legal advice.

## The process to build
1. A clear returns policy on the site, in plain English. Exceeding the legal minimum where
   it is cheap to do so builds trust.
2. A simple returns route — a pre-paid label (Royal Mail Tracked Returns, Evri Returns) or
   customer-paid. Decide and cost it in `state/financials.md`.
3. Restocking criteria — what can be resold, and what cannot.
4. Fast refunds — within the legal window, ideally faster. Slow refunds generate complaints
   and card chargebacks.

## The number
Track the return rate and the reasons. A high return rate on one product is product
feedback — sizing, description, or quality. Fix the cause, not just the logistics.
