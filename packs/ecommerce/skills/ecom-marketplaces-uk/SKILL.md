# Ecommerce — UK Marketplaces

A marketplace rents you an audience; an own store (see `ecom-shopify`) rents you nothing but
keeps the margin and the customer relationship. Comparing the two, and reading a fee
schedule, is validation-safe research — do it before Phase 1. Do not register a seller
account, list a product, or pay a subscription fee until the Phase 1 gate is met: ≥ 3 real
commitments (`AGENTS.md`).

## How to use this skill
1. Establish why a marketplace is even in the conversation — an existing audience to reach,
   a category shoppers search on the marketplace first (tools, homeware, fashion basics), or
   speed to first sale while the own store is not ready.
2. Pick ONE marketplace to test first. Match it to the product: Amazon UK for
   search-driven, commodity-adjacent products; Etsy for handmade, vintage, or craft supplies;
   eBay for used, refurbished, or auction-suited stock; TikTok Shop for products that sell on
   video demonstration and impulse.
3. Pull the current fee schedule for that one marketplace (see below) and run it through
   `financial-modeling-uk` — marketplace fees eat 10-30% of revenue before COGS, and the
   model must show a viable margin at that fee level.
4. Write listing copy and images to the platform's standards; keep titles honest — see the
   UK consumer-law points below before publishing.
5. Decide the fulfilment route (self, Amazon FBA, or marketplace-managed) and cost it — see
   `@docs/ECOM-OPS.md` for UK carrier rates if fulfilling yourself.
6. After the first real sales, review: marketplace vs, or alongside, an own store — see the
   comparison below — and log the decision.

## Marketplace vs an own store
A marketplace wins when the founder needs discovery the own store cannot yet buy or earn —
existing shopper intent, and little marketing budget to build traffic from scratch (check the
founder's real budget from the intake, `state/business-brief.md` section 5, rather than
assuming) `[ASSUMPTION — low risk]`. An own store wins once the venture has, or can build,
its own traffic (see `marketing-seo`,
`marketing-paid`), because the marketplace's fee and the loss of the customer relationship
(no owned email list, no repeat-purchase data) become the more expensive choice over time.
Many UK sellers run both — a marketplace for reach, a store for margin and brand — see
`packs/ecommerce/README.md` on packs being additive.

## Fee schedules (verify on the platform's current fee page before relying on these)
**All eBay UK and TikTok Shop business-seller fees below are quoted excluding VAT — add 20%
before modelling.** Amazon and Etsy figures already include VAT treatment as noted per line.

- **Amazon UK** — Individual plan: £0.75 per item, no monthly fee (worth it under ~35
  units/month). Professional plan: £25/month (excl. VAT), no per-item fee. On top of either,
  a referral fee applies per sale — commonly 15% of the sale price but ranging roughly 5-45%
  by category, with a £0.25 minimum per item. [Amazon Seller Central pricing, accessed
  September 2026 — verify on sell.amazon.co.uk/pricing]
- **Etsy** — £0.15 listing fee per item (live 4 months or until sold), 6.5% transaction fee
  on item price + shipping, Etsy Payments processing of 4% + £0.20, and a 0.25% regulatory
  operating fee; VAT at 20% is added to Etsy's own charges. Offsite Ads run at 15% of the
  attributed sale while the shop is below Etsy's threshold of US$10,000 of sales in the
  trailing 12 months (opt-out available), and drop to 12% but become mandatory once the shop
  passes it — the threshold is set in dollars, so convert at the current rate rather than
  trusting a sterling figure. [Etsy Fees & Payments Policy, accessed September 2026 — verify
  on etsy.com/uk/legal/fees]
- **eBay UK** — business sellers: final value fee of roughly 6.9%-12.8% of the total sale
  (item + postage) depending on category, with most categories at 10.9%. **All eBay UK
  business-seller fees are quoted excluding VAT — add 20% before modelling** (10.9% excl. =
  13.1% incl.). Plus a 0.35% regulatory operating fee (excl. VAT) and a per-order fee of 30p
  on orders up to £10 and 40p above £10 since 12 February 2026. Private sellers have paid no
  final value fee on most categories since October 2024, only the regulatory operating fee.
  [eBay UK fees pages / third-party summaries, accessed September 2026 — verify on
  ebay.co.uk/help/selling/fees-credits-invoices]
- **TikTok Shop UK** — around 9% platform commission as the standard rate, charged on the
  gross order value (product price plus customer-paid shipping, and often VAT), with no
  volume-discount tier. Some categories sit lower (~5%), and TikTok has revised UK rates
  more than once since launch — pull the category-by-category schedule from Seller Center
  rather than trusting a single figure. [Reported industry guides, accessed September 2026 —
  verify in TikTok Seller Center; TikTok does not publish a single definitive UK fee page]

## The Buy Box and reviews (Amazon)
Winning the Buy Box (the default "Add to Basket" button on a shared listing) is driven by
price, availability, fulfilment speed (FBA is favoured), and seller performance metrics —
not by being cheapest alone `[ASSUMPTION — medium risk; Amazon does not publish the exact
weighting]`. Reviews compound the same way as an own store, but incentivised or fake reviews
are now explicitly illegal: the Digital Markets, Competition and Consumers Act 2024 added
fake and undisclosed-incentivised reviews to the list of practices always considered unfair,
in force from 6 April 2025 (legislation.gov.uk / DMCC Act commencement regs, verify current
enforcement guidance on gov.uk before relying on it). Do not run a review-incentive scheme
without checking it against that rule first.

## UK consumer law and VAT points that bite marketplace sellers
- The Consumer Rights Act 2015 and the Consumer Contracts Regulations 2013 apply on a
  marketplace exactly as they do on an own store — a marketplace listing does not dilute the
  founder's obligations as the seller of record.
- If fulfilling from outside the UK, marketplaces have been legally required since 1 January
  2021 to charge and account for UK VAT on the founder's behalf for consignments of £135 or
  less sold to UK consumers (gov.uk guidance, "VAT and overseas goods sold directly to
  customers in the UK" — verify current thresholds before relying on this).
- The founder is still responsible for their own UK VAT registration and returns once the
  £90,000 threshold is approached — a marketplace collecting VAT on specific imports does not
  replace that; see `uk-tax-vat-mtd`.
- This is information, not legal or tax advice. Before relying on any of the above for a real
  listing or VAT decision, engage a UK chartered accountant or solicitor.
