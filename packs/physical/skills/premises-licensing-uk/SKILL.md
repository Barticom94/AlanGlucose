# Premises & Licensing (UK)

Phase 2 — MVP: once the venture needs a physical presence, its licences and lease terms sit
on the critical path to trading. Researching feasibility and cost is safe at Phase 1; signing
a lease, paying a licence fee, or committing to premises happens only after the Phase 1 gate
(≥ 3 real commitments) is met.

## How to use this skill
1. Confirm the gate first — check `state/progress.md`. Below the Phase 1 gate, treat this as
   research only: no applications, no deposits, no signatures.
2. Identify the premises model the venture actually needs: trading from home, a leased
   shop/unit, a market stall or street pitch, a shared kitchen/workshop, or a pop-up — the
   cheapest model that proves the model is usually right at this stage.
3. Check the planning use class of any specific address before treating it as viable (below) —
   a mismatch between the lease and the intended trade is expensive to unwind.
4. Work through the licence checklist below for the specific activity. Do not assume a figure
   or process — call the local authority's licensing team, since both vary by council.
5. Cost every rates bill (net of relief), licence fee, and insurance premium into
   `state/financials.md` before signing anything.
6. Get lease Heads of Terms and any personal guarantee reviewed by a solicitor before signing
   — this skill is not a substitute for that review.
7. Log the premises/licensing decision, and who reviewed it, in `state/decisions_log.md`.

## Trading from home
The cheapest starting point for most bootstrap physical ventures, and worth checking before
any lease or pitch. It has its own live issues:
- Check the mortgage or tenancy agreement for a business-use restriction before trading —
  breaching it can put the mortgage or tenancy at risk.
- Tell the home insurer. Business use — stock on the premises, customers visiting, deliveries
  — can invalidate a standard domestic policy if undisclosed.
- A material change of use may need planning permission from the local authority. The
  triggers are customer visits, signage, delivery frequency, and any staff working on site —
  not simply working from a laptop at the kitchen table.
- The part of the home used for business may be assessed separately for business rates by the
  Valuation Office Agency, alongside the normal council tax on the rest of the property.

## Leases — the basics
- Heads of Terms come first — a non-binding summary of rent, length, break clause, and any
  rent-free period. Get them in writing before instructing a solicitor.
- A full repairing and insuring (FRI) lease puts repair and insurance costs on the tenant. For
  an older building, negotiate a schedule of condition first so existing defects are not
  inherited as the tenant's liability.
- Landlords commonly ask a new limited company's director for a personal guarantee. Negotiate
  a cap, a time limit, or a company guarantee instead where possible — an uncapped guarantee
  has left directors personally liable for structural repairs.
- A shorter lease with a break clause usually costs more per month but caps the downside if
  the venture does not work — often the right trade at Phase 2.

## Business rates and relief
- In England, a property with a rateable value of £12,000 or less pays no business rates
  under Small Business Rate Relief, tapering to zero between £12,001 and £15,000 — but only
  where the business occupies a single property. With more than one property, relief survives
  only if no other property exceeds £2,899 rateable value and the total across all properties
  stays under £20,000 (£28,000 in London). Apply to the billing authority; do not assume it is
  applied automatically (gov.uk, "Business rates relief: small business rate relief",
  verified Sep 2026).
- From 1 April 2026 the multiplier depends on both rateable value and whether the property
  qualifies as retail, hospitality or leisure (RHL): under £51,000 RV — 43.2p standard, 38.2p
  if RHL; £51,000–£499,999 RV — 48p standard, 43p if RHL; £500,000+ RV — 50.8p (gov.uk,
  business rates information letter 2/2026, verified Sep 2026). Multipliers reset every
  April — verify the current figure on gov.uk before relying on it.
- Check the rateable value on the Valuation Office Agency site before signing — it also drives
  the fee band for a premises licence, below.
- Scotland and Wales run separate reliefs and multipliers (mygov.scot / businesswales.gov.wales)
  — do not assume the England figures apply.

## Planning — use classes
- Class E covers most shops, cafés, offices, and light services; moving between uses inside
  Class E usually needs no planning permission (Town and Country Planning (Use Classes)
  (Amendment) (England) Regulations 2020).
- Classes F.1/F.2 cover community and local uses — halls, places of worship, and small shops
  under 280m² selling essential goods.
- Sui generis uses — pubs, nightclubs, hot food takeaways, theatres — sit outside every class;
  moving into or out of one nearly always needs full planning permission.
- Confirm the current use class with the local planning authority before signing.

## Licences — check every one that applies
- **Food business registration** — free, with the local authority, at least 28 days before
  trading; required for any sale, preparation, or storage of food or drink, including from
  home or a stall (Food Standards Agency, gov.uk, verified Sep 2026).
- **Alcohol** — a premises licence (Licensing Act 2003) plus a personal licence for the
  designated premises supervisor. Personal licence application fee £37; premises licence fees
  are banded by rateable value, from £100 (Band A) to £635 or £1,905 for Band E if used mainly
  for on-sales — verify the current band and fee with the local authority's licensing team.
  Both are one-off application fees. A premises licence also carries an annual maintenance fee
  on the same bands — £70 (Band A) to £350 (Band E), doubled or trebled for alcohol-led
  premises (up to £1,050/year) — payable every year the licence is held (Licensing Act 2003
  fee regulations; verify the current schedule with the local authority). Model the annual
  fee, not just the application.
- **Street trading** — a local authority licence to trade from a stall, market, or the street.
  Fees vary enormously by council and pitch — examples range from roughly £150/year to several
  thousand pounds/year in prime locations [ASSUMPTION — figures are illustrative; verify the
  specific council's current fee].
- **Music** — playing recorded or live music (including radio) on the premises needs
  TheMusicLicence, a combined PRS for Music and PPL tariff; indicative 2026 costs (excl. VAT)
  range from roughly £240/year for a small salon to £450/year for a small shop, driven mainly
  by audible area — get a quote for the specific premises.
- **Late night refreshment** — supplying hot food or drink between 23:00 and 05:00 needs
  authorisation under the Licensing Act 2003, separate from any alcohol licence.
- **Pavement licence** — tables and chairs on the highway need a local-authority pavement
  licence (Business and Planning Act 2020); fees and conditions vary by council.
- Do not assume a licence transfers with a change of premises use or ownership; confirm with
  the local authority directly, since practice varies by council.

## Food Hygiene Rating Scheme
- In England, Wales, and Northern Ireland, the Food Standards Agency rates food premises 0
  (urgent improvement necessary) to 5 (very good) after inspection; the rating is public and
  searchable (food.gov.uk).
- Scotland runs a different scheme — the Food Hygiene Information Scheme — a Pass /
  Improvement Required rating, not the 0-5 scale.
- Treat the first inspection as a trading risk, not paperwork — a low score is public and
  costs trade. Prepare using the FSA's Safer Food Better Business pack before opening.

## Health, safety, and insurance
- Every business must make a "suitable and sufficient" risk assessment of risks to employees
  and anyone affected by the work (Management of Health and Safety at Work Regulations 1999,
  reg. 3) — this applies even to a sole trader with no staff, if the work could harm others.
- Employing 5 or more people adds two written duties from two different instruments: a
  written statement of general health and safety policy (Health and Safety at Work etc. Act
  1974, s.2(3), with the under-five exemption in the Employers' Health and Safety Policy
  Statements (Exception) Regulations 1975), and a written record of the significant findings
  of the risk assessment (Management of Health and Safety at Work Regulations 1999, reg. 3(6)).
- Public liability insurance is not a legal requirement but is close to essential once the
  public enters premises or handles a product — typical UK small-business premiums run
  roughly £70–£350/year for £1–2m of cover, depending on trade and claims history
  [ASSUMPTION — medium risk; get quotes rather than budgeting on this range alone].
- Employers' liability insurance IS a legal requirement (minimum £5m cover) the moment the
  venture has one employee — see `hiring-uk`.
