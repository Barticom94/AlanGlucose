# Services — Delivery Capacity

Phase 2 — MVP, once the Phase 1 gate is met. A booked pipeline the founder cannot actually
deliver is a worse problem than an empty one — it burns the founder and the client
relationship at the same time.

## Capacity, not "utilisation", is the real constraint
Industry benchmarks talk about a "utilisation rate" — the share of paid, full-time hours
that are billable. Solo consultants working full-time on their practice reportedly average
roughly 50–70% billable utilisation, well below the 70–85% typical of staffed consulting
firms, because business development, admin, and delivery all compete for the same hours
[ASSUMPTION — M — aggregated professional-services benchmark sources, 2026 — treat
as directional, not a target]. That benchmark assumes a full-time practice. For a founder
running this venture alongside other commitments, it does not transfer directly — start from
the founder's real weekly hours for the venture (the intake, section 5), and work out
delivery capacity from there, not from a full-time-consultant norm.

## How to use this skill
1. Get the founder's real available hours for the venture from `state/business-brief.md`
   (the intake, section 5) — do not assume a number.
2. Split those hours three ways: delivery (billable), sales/discovery, and admin
   (invoicing, proposals, running this brain). Delivery is rarely more than half of total
   hours for a founder still selling their own work [ASSUMPTION — L — heuristic, not a
   benchmark — replace with the founder's own logged data after the first two engagements].
3. Convert the productised offer's time-to-deliver (see `services-productised-offer`) into
   the number of concurrent clients the available delivery hours can actually support.
   Round down, not up — a slipped deadline costs more in trust than a client waiting a week
   longer for a start date.
4. Before accepting a client past that number, either free up capacity, subcontract, or say
   no and offer a later start date. A visible waitlist is a sales asset, not a failure.
5. Revisit the capacity number whenever the package, the founder's real hours, or the
   subcontractor bench changes — it is not a one-off calculation.

## Subcontract vs hire
- **Subcontract** — a named freelancer, paid per engagement, for overflow work or a
  specialist skill the founder does not have. Lower commitment, easier to reverse; the
  founder remains the client-facing contract holder and stays liable for the work delivered
  under their own terms — see `services-proposals-contracts-uk` — check whether the
  arrangement is labour-only before assuming no employers' liability cover is needed
  (`docs/SERVICES-OPS.md`, Insurance).
- **Hire** — a Phase 4 decision only, once revenue is repeatable and a bottleneck is proven
  rather than felt — see `hiring-uk` for the PAYE, IR35, and employment mechanics.
- Default to subcontracting first for a services business: the workload is naturally lumpy,
  and a subcontractor carries no payroll obligation.

## Client onboarding and offboarding
- **Onboarding** — a short, repeatable checklist: contract signed, deposit received, kickoff
  call booked, access and credentials exchanged, first milestone dated. Write it as an SOP
  once it repeats (see `ops-sops`) so quality does not depend on memory.
- **Offboarding** — a deliberate close, not a quiet stop: final handover, outstanding
  invoice cleared, a named review point, and an explicit ask for a referral or testimonial
  while the value delivered is freshest. A client who is not offboarded well rarely refers.

## Referrals — the primary channel
- A service business's cheapest and highest-trust channel is a referral from a satisfied
  client, not paid acquisition — see `marketing-paid` for why paid channels are usually the
  wrong Phase 2 spend for a services business.
- Ask for the referral explicitly, at the point of a delivered result (offboarding, above),
  not passively. "Do you know one other person who has this problem?" outperforms "let me
  know if you know anyone."
- Track the referral source for every new client in `state/financials.md`. If referrals are
  not arriving, that is signal about delivery quality, not just about asking often enough.

## Output
Update `state/financials.md` with concurrent-client capacity and the referral source for
each new client. Log any subcontract-vs-hire decision, and any capacity ceiling reached, in
`state/decisions_log.md`.
