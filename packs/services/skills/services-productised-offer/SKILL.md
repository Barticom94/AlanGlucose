# Services — Productised Offer

Phase 1 onward for defining the deliverable, the scope boundary, and a price hypothesis — a
buyer cannot commit to an offer with no name and no number. Nothing is printed, built, or
paid for before the Phase 1 gate (≥ 3 real commitments): a polished offer deck for an
unvalidated service is still guesswork wearing a suit.

## Why productise
An open-ended "I do consulting" is unsellable — a buyer cannot picture what they get or what
it costs, so they stall. A productised offer names the problem solved, the deliverable, the
timeline, and the price before the first sales conversation starts. It also lets the founder
quote fast and deliver consistently instead of re-scoping every engagement from a blank page.

## How to use this skill
1. Take the discovery interviews (see `customer-discovery`) and find the one problem that
   came up most often, described in the customer's own words — not the founder's.
2. Name the deliverable precisely: what the client receives, in what format, by when. "I'll
   help with marketing" is not a package; "a 90-day paid-search launch: account build,
   creative brief, and a written handover report" is.
3. Draw the scope boundary explicitly — list what is included and, just as importantly,
   what is excluded. Every unstated exclusion becomes a future change-request the founder
   absorbs for free.
4. Choose the commercial model:
   - **Fixed price** — best when the scope is genuinely repeatable; the founder carries the
     delivery-time risk, so pad the time estimate.
   - **Day rate** — simplest to quote, but caps upside and rewards slow delivery; the
     weakest fit for a productised offer.
   - **Retainer** — recurring revenue and a predictable pipeline; needs an explicit monthly
     scope cap or it quietly becomes unlimited free work.
5. Price from the value the outcome creates for the client (their cost of the problem, or
   the revenue it unlocks for them) — not from hours worked. See `pricing-strategy` for the
   method and the willingness-to-pay test.
6. Test the packaged offer on real prospects before treating it as final. A price or scope
   nobody accepts is a hypothesis, not a finished product.
7. Land the first three clients as evidence, not as a favour from friends — see
   `sales-playbook`, and treat each one against the Phase 1 gate defined in
   `customer-discovery`.

## Common package shapes
Three shapes cover most services businesses; pick the one the discovery evidence points to,
rather than defaulting to whichever feels most comfortable to sell:
- **Diagnostic / audit** — a short, low-price, low-risk entry package (a review, an
  assessment, a plan) that lets a new client try the founder before committing to the main
  engagement. Its job is to convert strangers into clients, not to be profitable on its own.
- **Core project** — the main productised offer: a defined outcome, delivered once, priced
  to reflect the value created. This is usually where the real margin sits.
- **Retainer** — ongoing work for an existing client once trust is established. Easier to
  sell to someone who has already seen the founder deliver than to a stranger.
A founder does not need all three on day one — the core project alone is enough to clear the
Phase 1 gate. Add the diagnostic once lead volume is the bottleneck, and the retainer once
delivery quality is proven.

## Scope creep — the productised offer's main threat
Every "quick add" outside the stated deliverable erodes the margin the price was built on.

## UK specifics
- VAT: once turnover crosses **£90,000** on a rolling 12-month basis, registration is
  mandatory, and a B2C price either absorbs the 20% or must rise — model this before setting
  a price that anticipates growth near the threshold (HMRC VAT registration threshold,
  unchanged at Spring Statement 2026 — verify on gov.uk).
- If quoting a fixed price to a VAT-registered business client, state plainly whether the
  price is inclusive or exclusive of VAT — B2B buyers expect exclusive by default.
- Income-tax admin bites before VAT — see `uk-tax-vat-mtd` for the MTD ITSA £50,000
  threshold.
